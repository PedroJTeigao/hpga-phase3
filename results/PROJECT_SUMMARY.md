## 1. Abstract

The project began with Xue et al.'s bottleneck model for island-model parallel GAs and asked what happens to it when the genetic operators are LLM calls; it widened into whether LLM operators and multi-agent structures improve search at all. On the classical baseline the model held in order of magnitude, not numerically (predicted DIS ceiling C≈2.2 after redefining `T_interval`, observed 3.09–3.32x [P1 §4]); LLM operators then move the serial fraction from 8.9% [P1 §4] to 99.95% [P2 §6], leaving worker count nothing to act on (N=8 speedup 1.078 [P2 §2]); parallel dispatch of operator calls reached 1.00–1.23x against an ideal of 2–8x on this hardware, with the mechanism not isolated [P2 §7.3–7.4]. A prompt format that removes the option of copying (`position`/`segment`) brings fallback to 0 at three alphabet sizes (0/400 and 0/100 at 20 letters [P3 §9.1]), but the operators then use a small part of their space: 20 of 63 mutation positions and 1–2 of 99 cut values [P3 §9.2–9.3]. Agent and circle structures produce reliably higher population diversity (1.45x–5.95x [P3 §2, §6.2]) that did not become a fitness lead on the lattice [P3 §7.3], and in sequence mode circles did no better than random immigrants (D above E in 1 of 5 seeds [SEQ §9.0]). On TM-score fitness, GA with LLM operators beat random search in 5 of 5 seeds (+0.0862 mean) but was not distinguishable from GA with deterministic operators (3 of 5 seeds) [SEQ §1], at 4.0–5.4x the wall time [SEQ §4]. With 5 seeds the smallest reachable p is 0.0625 [SEQ §1], so no sequence-mode comparison is significant, and parallel scaling was never measured in sequence mode.

A final, decisive round then asked whether giving an operator more information, or direct access to the real fitness function, changes any of this. Three agent-memory arms (private record of an agent's own past edits: none, shown as prose, shown as two explicit lists) confirmed the mechanism worked exactly as designed — the explicit-list format took the agent-propose fallback rate from a worst-seed 20/38 to 0/38 and the rate of returning to a self-labelled-worse position from 37% to 0% over 255 pooled edits [AM3, AM3B; ODM Evidence 2] — and still left all three arms statistically indistinguishable on fitness [AM3B §2; ODM]. A greedy oracle (O) with direct read access to the real fitness function then did not merely fail to beat plain random injection: it was the worst of five arms, beaten 3 of 3 seeds by two cheaper arms [AM3C §2; ODM Evidence 3], because its within-step search — the best of 8 candidate single-position mutations gains only 0.0075–0.0206 TM, and none of the 8 beats the current genome in 53–61% of steps — consumes real evaluation budget that the other arms spend on roughly twice as many breeding steps instead [AM3C §1c; ODM Evidence 4–5]. With no LLM anywhere, four move classes then changed how often a single move improves on its base (S4 more often than every other arm, and S2 more often than S1, S3 and the baseline operator B, in every seed), but none of them beat another on final fitness; the only ordering in every seed was B over single substitution, at three seeds and not significant [MC]. A screen then asked whether a protein language model could rank those same moves: scoring all 3,990 move-class moves with ESM-2 150M's masked likelihood, the model's preference did not predict realised fitness gain — moves it preferred improved 31.6% of the time against 31.3% for moves it disliked, and sign agreement was 49.6%, a coin flip [E2S]. Read together with the four earlier operator interventions, the natural reading is that on this problem, at this budget, the choice of perturbation is not what determines search quality; something else, most plausibly selection across generations, is doing the work [ODM].

## 2. The original question

The base paper is Xue et al. (NoCS 2014). Its bottleneck model (Eq. 3/7) gives the speedup ceiling of the fitness-dispatch (DIS) phase, and its DIBM model (Eq. 11) says capacity and speedup scale with P borrowed injection channels [P2 §2, §7]. Phase 1 re-derived it as a Python master/slave HPGA [P1 §1] and found it transfers in order of magnitude but not in its constants [P1 §4, §6]. Phase 2 put LLM calls in place of crossover and mutation for one stated reason: "to put `T_calc`/`T_turnaround`-scale latency into a large, realistic regime and measure what that does to the bottleneck model," not to improve search, and it predicted no semantic advantage on a flat string of move symbols [P2 §5].

The question widened in three steps, each documented in the file that ends the previous one. P2 §8 added persistent-identity agents and communication. P3 added circles and a blackboard, and ended by arguing that the diversity-to-fitness question could not be settled on a representation with no semantic content, naming amino-acid sequences scored by ESMFold as the critical path [P3 §8]. SEQ then asks the search question directly: random search vs GA vs GA with LLM operators (A–C, [SEQ §1]), and circles vs random immigrants (D, E, [SEQ §9]). A fourth step (branch `agent-memory`) asked two more pointed questions of the same design: does giving an agent operator *more information* — a private record of its own past edits and their outcome — change what it does and how well it does; and does giving an operator *direct access to the real fitness function*, the most information any operator could have, do any better than the LLM operators or plain random injection at the same evaluation budget. ODM is the standalone answer to both.

## 3. What each phase built, measured and established

### 3.1 Phase 1: classical HPGA baseline, Eq. 3/7

**Built:** a single-island master/slave HPGA for 2D HP-lattice folding in Python `multiprocessing`, a software re-derivation of Xue et al.'s bottleneck analysis (Section III.A); the master holds the population and dispatches to N persistent worker processes [P1 §1]. **Measured:** population 256, 40 generations, N ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 32} on a 6-physical/12-logical-core machine, timing on a synthetic 300-residue sequence, GA correctness separately on Unger & Moult n=20 (E\*=-9) [P1 §1].

**Key numbers** (3-repeat batch, mean ± sd):
- GA validation: exact optimum in 3 of 5 seeds (5 seeds × 500 generations), the other 2 one contact short, all five folds valid, converged by generation ~25–50 [P1 §2].
- DIS-phase speedup 1.777 ± 0.048 (N=2), 2.760 ± 0.151 (N=4), a peak of 3.319 ± 0.122 at N=8, then 3.120 (N=12), 2.913 (N=16), 2.591 ± 0.234 (N=32). Overall speedup peaks at 2.695 ± 0.093 at N=8. Worker utilisation falls monotonically from 0.619 to 0.100 and `T_calc` grows from 0.201 to 0.486 ms, which P1 reads as core contention beyond ~6 workers [P1 §3].
- Model: taking `T_interval` literally as the `queue.put()` call gives a ceiling C≈190, about 60x above what was observed; redefining it as the master's total serial cost per individual gives C≈2.2, against observed DIS peaks of 3.09–3.32x (original run and repeat batch). The finite-N form 1+C≈3.2 is presented "as an approximate scale for this regime, not a strict bound": recomputed from the batch's own baseline it is 3.06–3.21, and the observed peak exceeds it by 3.5–8.6% [P1 §4].
- Serial GA phase ≈ 8.9% of N=1 wall time [P1 §4].

**P1's own limitations:** session-level timing variance of several percent (`T_calc`(N=1) 0.2144 ms in the original run vs 0.201 ms; overall peak 2.50x vs 2.695x) [P1 §3, §5]; N=8 and N=12 are not statistically separable at n=3, so the supported claim is "Saturates around N=8–12", not "at N=8" [P1 §5]; the ceiling derivation assumes no worker/master overlap at N=1, which the queue's feeder threads may violate, an untested explanation for why the ceiling is exceeded [P1 §5]. The timing workload never converges (fitness -73) by design [P1 §2]. Derivations, raw-data pointers and the correction history are in `PHASE1_SUMMARY.md`, which is not in this repository [P1 header].

**Established:** a validated classical baseline with an instrumented harness. The two-bottleneck framing (a dispatch bottleneck plus a serial GA phase that caps overall speedup by Amdahl's law) transfers in kind, with core contention standing in for channel bandwidth; the paper's constants "do not transfer literally" [P1 §4, §6].

### 3.2 Phase 2: LLM operators, serial fraction, latency, affordance (`gemma4:12b`, Q4_K_M)

**Built:** `HPGA_OPERATOR_MODE=llm` swaps crossover and mutation for Ollama calls; the rest of the harness is byte-identical to Phase 1 [P2 §1, RM]. **Measured** (details in findings 2–6, 8, 9): a paired pilot and N-sweep at `pop_size=8`, a 33-call latency fit, a diff-style prompt, operator reliability and coverage, parallel operator dispatch timed on `llama3.2:1b`, and a 3-arm agent run. Key numbers: GA phase 305.26 s of 305.42 s wall (99.95%), N=8 speedup 1.078 [P2 §2]; an output token costs ~63x an input token, R²=0.991 (I refit the fit from the raw file and got the same constants) [P2 §3]; dispatch speedup at most 1.23x [P2 §7.3]; reliability and coverage numbers are in findings 4–6. Also the 3D model check: plain 3D HP on CPSP-tools S1 (n=27, proven E\*=-22), best GA result 20 over 5 seeds, validation not passed, external structure check unfinished; the base paper uses HPSC, which this model is not [RM]. **Established:** worker count is stranded under LLM operators; free-form operators are unreliable, and position/segment operators are reliable but low-variance.

### 3.3 Phase 3: circles, blackboard, diversity gap (lattice)

**Built:** circles of agents that consult within a circle, and a blackboard that carries observations, never genomes, across circles [P3 §1]. **Measured:** population diversity, identical-parent rate, and fitness, on an anchor run (length 18, 1 seed) and on length 24 with 3 seeds at 10 and 15 generations. Key numbers: diversity ratios 1.45x–3.16x over six runs and 5.95x (12.97 vs 2.18) at length 24 [P3 §2, §6.2]; final fitness 3.33 vs 3.67 at 10 generations and 3.67 vs 4.00 at 15, seed 1 an outright loss [P3 §6.3, §7.2]; 1.89x more tokens per fitness point and ~4.8x wall time [P3 §6.4, §6.6]. By-products (crossover gate, proxy miss) are in findings 17 and §5. **Established:** a robust diversity effect and no demonstrated fitness benefit at roughly 2x token cost; P3 §8 concluded the representation, not the architecture, was the limit.

### 3.4 P3 §9: 20-letter operator coverage and the segment ablation (no fitness, no GA)

**Built/measured:** the sequence `GenomeModel`'s LLM operators called in isolation, `gemma4:12b`, temperature 0.7, seed 0, each figure a single run [P3 §9]. Key numbers: fallback 0/400 (`position`) and 0/100 (`segment`) against 24/30 and 26/30 for the `full` styles [P3 §9.1]; mutate reached 20 of 63 positions, one alone 37.3% [P3 §9.2]; crossover used 1–2 of 99 possible cuts in every one of four 100-call prompt settings [P3 §9.3; raw `segment_example_ablation_summary.json`, checked]. **Established:** the format effect and the low-variance behaviour both carry to 20 letters; the 40 came from the prompt's prose number, not the worked example.

### 3.5 Sequence GA: TM-score fitness, arms A–E, five seeds

**Setup.** Fitness is the TM-score of the ESMFold structure against 7UR7 chain A (63 residues), normalised by reference length; the native sequence scores 0.9137 against itself [SEQ header; raw `tm_fitness_sanity_console.log`: 0.91366]. ESMFold runs fp32 on a 15360 MiB T4 (fp16 gives 100% NaN; fp32 OOMs at 300 residues) [ESM §3–4]. Population 16, 20 generations, ~275–282 distinct folds for A–C; D and E ran 342–358 because their 4 injected genomes per step add folds, so every comparison is read at the common per-seed count (275–280) [SEQ §2, §9.1]. Arms: A random search; B GA, deterministic operators; C GA, LLM operators (`position` mutate, `segment` crossover); D = C plus circles ported to sequences; E = C plus 4 random immigrants per step, the control for D [SEQ §2, §9].

### 3.6 Agent memory and the oracle upper bound: five arms, one budget, seeds 0–2

**Setup.** Same GA design as §3.5 (pop 16, 20 evaluated populations, 19 breeding steps, `gemma4:12b`), same evaluation-budget discipline (every comparison read at the smallest distinct-fold count reached by any arm in that seed). Five arms, additive to the ordinary fill exactly as D/E were: **M1** 2 persistent agents, each with a private record of its own past position edits and their fitness outcome, kept but never shown; **M2** the same, shown as prose ("round N: position p old→new; fitness X → Y"); **M2b** the same real record, shown as two explicit lists ("Do not change these positions…" / "These positions improved before…" — the format two isolated probes, 100 calls/condition each, showed the model obeys almost perfectly where it does not obey prose: gate TV(A,B)=0.382 vs a null of 0.306, p=0.0016; format probe avoid-share 6%→0% (explicit) vs 6%→24% — the wrong direction — (prose), prefer-share 3%→96% (explicit) vs 3%→3% (prose, unmoved), raw `agent_memory_gate.json`, `agent_memory_format.json`); **M3** 2 random immigrants, the control (as E); **O** 2 slots run by a greedy oracle, no LLM — each tries 8 single-position mutations per breeding step, scores every one with the real fitness function through the same cache-aware evaluator the rest of the population uses (charged to the same distinct-fold budget), and keeps the best of {current genome, the 8 candidates}, never accepting a worse move. Seeds 0–2 for every arm.

Among M1/M2/M2b/M3, no arm beats any other (largest: M1 vs M2, 1 of 3 seeds, mean +0.0359; M1 vs M2b tied in one seed, mean +0.0019) [AM3B §2]. **M1 beats O 3 of 3 (mean +0.0795); M2b beats O 3 of 3 (mean +0.0776); M3 does not formally beat O (2 of 3) but leads it by +0.0622 on the mean** [AM3C §2]. O's own diagnostics: it kept its current genome (no candidate improved on it) in 53–61% of steps, moved in the rest with a mean gain of 0.0075–0.0206 TM when it did, spent 288 real fitness evaluations per run on that search, and at the common budget had completed only ~9–10 of the other arms' 19 breeding steps [AM3C §1c; recomputed against `agent_memory_O_seed*.json`]. The memory mechanism itself worked exactly as designed: read as a share of the edits that land on any position in the agent's own record at all (the fair, format-probe-matched comparison — M1 touches such a position in 73% of edits, M2 in 56%, M2b in 7%), M1 splits those touches roughly evenly between "better"- and "worse"-labelled (49%/51%), M2 skews toward "worse" (41%/59%), and M2b's rare in-record touches are almost entirely on "better"-labelled positions (89%/0%) — in raw counts, 0 of M2b's 255 pooled edits landed on a "worse" position, against M1's 95 and M2's 57 [AM3B §6; ODM Evidence 2]. The same directional effect, cleanly signed because every call there is paired against a matched no-record null, appears in the isolated Stage 2 gate: landings on "better"-labelled positions fell from 30/98 to 8/98 under a prose record (p=0.0002) and landings on "worse"-labelled ones rose from 30/98 to 47/98 (p=0.016) [raw `agent_memory_gate.json`; ODM Evidence 2]. M2b's explicit-list record also dropped agent-propose fallback from a worst-seed 20/38 to 0/38 in every seed [AM3, AM3B §1b]. **Established:** the memory mechanism is real and precisely characterised, but it does not move fitness in either direction; direct fitness access does not beat random injection either, and here does measurably worse, because its own evaluation cost buys fewer generations of ordinary selection at the same total budget. ODM is the full standalone account, including the isolated probes, the exact per-seed numbers, and the stated scope of the claim.

### 3.7 Move class: four deterministic move classes plus the baseline operator, no LLM, seeds 0–2

*Sources for this section: **MC** = `results/MOVE_CLASS.md`, **MCT** = `results/MOVE_CLASS_TABLES.md` (generated from `results/raw/move_class_*.json`).*

**Setup.** ODM Evidence 4 measured the oracle's candidate set, which was single-position by construction, so it is a statement about the move class, not about who chooses the move [MC question]. This experiment holds the chooser fixed and varies only the move class, with **no LLM anywhere** (`HPGA_OPERATOR_MODE=deterministic`); everything else is §3.5's arm B unchanged (pop 16, 20 evaluated populations, 19 breeding steps, tournament 3, elitism 2, crossover 0.9) [MC Design]. Each of the 14 non-elite children per breeding step gets exactly one move of the arm's class: **S1** one substitution; **S2** 2–5 substitutions; **S3** one segment of 5–15 residues refilled at random; **S4** one insertion or deletion of 1–5 residues. **B**, the reference row, is the existing per-site operator (p = 0.05, about 3 substitutions per child), called unchanged. S1 is a new arm, not the baseline, and Evidence 4 was measured on the oracle's candidates, not on B [MC Design]. A move's payoff needs its base (the post-crossover child), which the GA never folds; those bases were folded after each run, off-budget, into separate files. Two checks per run showed that measuring changed nothing: a digest of every GA output before and after measurement, and a replay with recording off served only from the run's own cache. Both passed in all 15 runs, and the three B runs reproduce `sequence_ga_cmp_B_seed{0,1,2}.json` exactly [MC Verification]. Measurement cost 2,493 extra folds: 1.74 fold-hours against 2.90 for the GA's own folds, 37.5% of fold time (32.5–40.2% per arm). These are fold wall-clock times; nothing in the files records GPU utilisation [MC Verification].

On fitness, **only B beats S1 (3 of 3); no move class beats any other**, and the best arm changes with the seed (S4 in seed 0, S2 in seed 1, S3 in seed 2) [MC §1; MCT §1]. S2's 0.6522 in seed 1 is the highest single run in the project, 0.015 above D seed 1 (0.6372), and does not stand apart from earlier runs [MC §1]. On per-move payoff, in every seed, **S4 improves on its base more often than every other arm, S2 more often than S1, S3 and B, and S2, S3 and S4 each gain more than S1 when they improve** [MC §3]. Two qualifications come from the per-seed thirds: S4 is higher than B in 8 of 9 seed-by-third cells (the exception is early seed 1, B 40/84 against S4 34/84) but strictly highest of all five arms in only 5 of 9; and S2 is not higher than B in the late third in any seed (tied at 29/98, then 27/98 against 28/98, and 30/98 against 32/98) [MC §4]. On expected gain per move (hit rate × gain when improved), pooled, S1 is lowest, 0.0067 against 0.0092–0.0118, but not in seed 2, where S3 is lower [MC conclusion]. *Interpretation, not a demonstrated fact:* the move class changes the hit rate without changing the outcome, which is consistent with the evaluation budget, not the quality of the move, being the binding constraint on final fitness; the same pattern would also arise if the pooled hit-rate differences behind those orderings (2.9 to 9.8 percentage points) are too small to show through seed-to-seed variance in three runs, and the data cannot tell these two readings apart [MC conclusion]. Bases are not matched across arms, so part of any payoff difference is a difference in what was being mutated [MC conclusion]. **Observed, not significant:** move class changes how often a single move improves on its base, in the same direction in every seed, and pooled, single substitution is the least productive move; neither turned into a fitness ordering among move classes at this budget.

### 3.8 Does a protein language model's preference predict fitness? A screen, not an arm

*Source for this section: **E2S** = `results/ESM2_LIKELIHOOD_SCREEN.md`.*

**Setup.** The next arm under consideration was ESM-2 as the operator: instead of an LLM choosing edits from prose, use a protein language model's own likelihoods to propose mutations. That arm rests on an assumption testable **before building anything**, because §3.7's bases files already hold, for 3,990 moves, the base genome, the child genome and both fitnesses. **No GA was run and no model proposed any move** — this scores moves the deterministic move classes already made, which makes them an unbiased sample with respect to the model, a stricter test than scoring moves it chose itself. Model: `facebook/esm2_t30_150M_UR50D`, 148,140,154 parameters, fp32; every score is a **masked** likelihood (position *i* replaced by `<mask>` and predicted from the rest), logits restricted to the 20 canonical letters. Cost: 6,446 unique genomes (3,144 bases plus 3,978 children, deduplicated), 6,086 s on an idle T4 [E2S].

Two quantities. **Δlogit** is the sum over the positions a move changed of `logit(new) − logit(old)` in the base's masked context — what an operator would consult — defined only where base and child align, i.e. the substitution arms (3,192 moves). **ΔPLL** is `PLL(child) − PLL(base)` over whole sequences, which needs no positional correspondence and so covers every arm including S4's 798 indels, all of which change length; S4 was therefore **not** excluded [E2S].

| metric | n | pooled Pearson r | moves the model prefers (Δ>0) that improved | moves it dislikes (Δ<0) that improved | sign agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Δlogit | 3192 | +0.025 | 514/1626 (31.6%) | 480/1533 (31.3%) | 49.6% |
| ΔPLL | 3990 | −0.020 | 630/1981 (31.8%) | 668/1976 (33.8%) | 49.0% |

**The model's preference does not predict fitness here.** The pooled correlations are about 0.05% of variance; the per-arm signs disagree on ΔPLL (S1 −0.082, S3 −0.049 and S4 −0.087 against S2 +0.101 and B +0.098), which is what noise looks like rather than a relationship; and on ΔPLL the decision-relevant gap runs the *wrong* way. For scale, sorting moves by ESM-2's preference separates them less than the choice of move class does (§3.7's hit rates span 30.7% to 38.1%) [E2S §1–4].

A weaker question does show something: a base genome's mean per-position log-likelihood against its own fitness is +0.146 pooled over 3,197 unique bases (p~1e-16). But it is 2% of variance, **absent in seed 0** (+0.058, p~0.061) while present in seeds 1 (+0.160) and 2 (+0.087), and partly a trajectory artifact — both likelihood and fitness rise with breeding step (+0.118 and +0.460), and conditioning on step leaves +0.177 early and +0.173 late but +0.004 in the middle third. By this project's every-seed rule it is not a demonstrated effect, and it is a statement about genomes, not about ranking moves [E2S §5]. Calibration: the native 7UR7 chain A scores −2.253 mean per-position LL, the 3,197 evolved bases average −3.111, and **0 of 3,197 score above the native sequence** — the whole measurement sits in a band the model finds uniformly unlikely [E2S §5].

p-values here are a two-sided normal approximation on Fisher's z, **uncorrected for multiple comparisons, with 20 tests** (5 arms × 2 metrics × 2 coefficients); sign consistency across arms is what to read, not any individual p [E2S].

**Established:** ESM-2 150M's masked likelihood carries essentially no information about realised TM-score change for these moves on this target. **Not established:** that an ESM-2-*proposed* move would fail — an argmax operator proposes the extreme selected tail of the distribution measured here, and a flat correlation over the bulk is compatible with that tail behaving differently. No decision about the arm follows from this document.

## 5. Negative results, stated plainly

**Did not work, or no demonstrated benefit.**
- LLM operators vs deterministic operators in sequence mode: C higher in 3 of 5 seeds; without seed 3, C's mean difference from B is -0.0064 [SEQ §4]. On the lattice, the LLM crossover reproduced the midpoint single-point cut the deterministic operator already makes [P2 §5].
- Circles: no fitness edge over C or over random immigrants [SEQ §9.0], and on the lattice no widening lead with more generations [P3 §7.2]. Adding seed 4 shrank D's mean edge over C from +0.0135 to +0.0037 and over E from +0.0184 to +0.0053 [SEQ §9.13]. In the five-arm comparison, D holds both the best single run (0.6372, seed 1) and the worst of the LLM arms (0.3587, seed 0) [SEQ §9.0]. Move-class S2 seed 1 later reached 0.6522, 0.015 higher [MC §1].
- Communication paid nothing and refine added zero distinct genomes [P2 §8]. Diff-style crossover made things worse [P2 §3]. The affordance fix failed on `llama3.2:1b` [P2 §7.1].
- The 3D model's validation did not pass (20 of 22) and its external check is unfinished [RM].
- Phase 1's model constants did not transfer: the literal `T_interval` reading overshoots the observed ceiling ~60x, the redefined one gives C≈2.2 against a 3.09–3.32x peak, and the observed peak exceeds 1+C by 3.5–8.6% [P1 §4].
- Proxies mispredicted: the deterministic sweep put the plateau at generation ~54, reality ~7 [P3 §6.5]; ESM's lattice proxy predicted a 32.8% revisit rate [ESM §5] and the sequence runs had 11.9–14.1% cache hits (recomputed from `n_cache_hits`, B and C; a different config, so not like-for-like).
- Neither memory format (M2 prose, M2b explicit lists) moved fitness relative to no memory (M1) or to each other, despite M2b's format mechanism confirmed working exactly as designed [AM3B §2; ODM]. A greedy oracle with direct access to the real fitness function did not beat random injection either — it was the worst of five arms and was beaten outright by two of the other four [AM3C §2; ODM Evidence 3]. A protein language model's preference does not rank moves on this landscape either: over all 3,990 move-class moves, ESM-2 150M's preferred moves improved 31.6% of the time against 31.3% for the ones it disliked, with sign agreement at 49.6% [E2S §3]. That is a screen on moves the model did not propose, not a failed arm (§3.8).

**Claims withdrawn or shrunk.**
- **Identical-parent feedback loop.** The off/on identical-parent ratio was 2.7x while an operator was broken and 1.2x once both worked (0.200 vs 0.167; 1.51x call-weighted); a middle 1.65x figure has unknown provenance and is excluded. "A hypothesis that looked strong and shrank" [P3 §3]. I recomputed 0.200 and 0.1667 from `circles_smoke_summary_1789603059.json`.
- **Example anchoring.** The 40-mode was not produced by the worked example: with the example moved to 70, 40 stayed modal (74%) and 70 was never chosen; with no example, 77%. This does not show the example could never matter [P3 §9.3].
- **Contention as the explanation of the flat dispatch curve (a caveat, not a withdrawal).** P2 §7.3.2 and §7.4 now say the flat curve is "consistent with" contention for the shared GPU (§7.4: "consistent with the data but not established"), while the static memory wall (P=1 baseline 79.96 s vs 35.52 s) is measured directly [P2 §7.2]. Contention was never measured directly: the `pop_size=32` run shows only that removing the structural fan-out ceiling did not restore scaling, and the section's concurrency probe, on tiny 16-token requests, reached 6.98x at K=8 on the same GPU and model [P2 §7.1–7.3]. Treated here as unproven.
- **"Concurrent dispatch is actively harmful."** The single-run reading (0.80x at P=4, 0.81x at P=8) did not reproduce with 3 repeats; that P=1 draw was ~2.4 sd above the repeated mean [P2 §7.3.1].
- **Other users' load as the cause of an operator timeout.** Withdrawn by the project lead: the timeout was model cold-start. No committed file records the claim or its withdrawal, so this is stated on the lead's word, not from a source; SEQ §6 item 13 says only that cold-start effects were not excluded from any timing.
- "Circles reached fitness 2.0 faster" is not carried as an advantage: both arms plateau at the same value [P3 §6.3].

**Where sources disagree.** Earlier inconsistencies (P3 §6.3 vs §7.3 scoreboard, the P3 §8 ratio count, the README's git note, P2 §7.4's contention wording) were corrected in commits `6ef0675` and `7836b92`. One remains: P1 §4 puts the serial GA phase at ~8.9% of N=1 wall time, while P1 §3's own DIS/GA ratio at N=1 (10.56 ± 0.26) implies 8.65% (computed as 1/(1+10.56)); P1 does not say how the two relate, and P2 §6 quotes 8.9%.

## 6. Limitations and gaps

- **Parallel scaling was not measured in sequence mode.** The sequence drivers run in-process with one worker, outside `Island`: ESMFold is ~13.7 GB in fp32 and fits on the GPU once, so it cannot be replicated per worker [`experiments/run_sequence_ga_smoke.py` docstring; `experiments/run_sequence_ga_comparison.py` docstring; SEQ §6 item 8]. Ollama (~8 GB) and ESMFold also cannot share the 15 GB T4, so arms C, D, E swap them, costing 7–15% of wall time [SEQ §3 tbl 6, §9.7]. The only scaling data are lattice ones [P1 §3, P2 §2, §7.3], on different hardware (a 6-physical-core CPU machine in Phase 1 [P1 §1], a 4 GB GTX 1650 Ti in P2 §7, a T4 for the sequence work [ESM]).
- **Small populations.** `pop_size=8` (lattice), 16 (sequence), 20 generations, ~275–282 distinct folds per run [SEQ §2].
- **One model.** `gemma4:12b` at temperature 0.7 [SEQ §5]; `llama3.2:1b` only as a timing instrument [P2 §7.0]. The latency fit is at temperature 0, the operators run at 0.7 [P2 §5].
- **Phase 1 timing.** Three repeats, one machine, C≈2.2 resting on a redefinition of `T_interval`; see §3.1 [P1 §3–5].
- **Seeds.** 5 at most in the sequence work; 3 on the lattice, 1 for the anchor, single runs in P2 §4 and §8; 3 for the memory and oracle arms (AM3/AM3B/AM3C); 3 for the move-class arms (MC). With 5, the smallest two-sided sign-test p is 0.0625; with 3, 0.25 — so nothing in SEQ, AM3/AM3B/AM3C or MC is significant at 0.05 [SEQ §1; AM3B §2; MC].
- **One target.** A single 63-residue reference; the native sequence scores 0.9137, which is the ceiling [SEQ §5]. The memory and oracle arms use the same target and fitness function; no other target was tried [ODM Scope].
- **No attribution.** No ablation of selection, crossover or mutation, so B's and C's edge over A cannot be assigned to an operator [SEQ §5]. The oracle result (finding 20–21) is consistent with "selection is doing the work" but does not directly test it either — no arm here holds crossover/mutation fixed and varies only selection [ODM Limits].
- **Budget definition.** Equal distinct folds hides C's ~5x wall time [SEQ §5]; C's swap accounting differs from D/E's (its Ollama reload, est. 139–175 s per run, sits in LLM time) [SEQ §9.7]. The oracle's own within-step evaluations are charged to the same distinct-fold budget by design (the point of that arm), which is also why it completes only ~half the other arms' breeding steps at the common cut [AM3C §1c; ODM Evidence 5].
- **Model mismatch.** Plain 3D HP, not the base paper's HPSC [RM]; P2 §7 numbers come from the 2D 3-symbol model [P2 §7].
- **One move class for the oracle.** 8 single-position candidate mutations per slot per step, not swept; a wider search or a richer move class (multi-position edits, structural moves) was not tried, so the oracle result bounds this move class at this width, not operator design in general [ODM Limits]. Multi-position, segment and indel moves were since tested in a plain GA with no LLM (§3.7), where they changed the hit rate but not final fitness, but not as the oracle's candidates, so the oracle question stays open [MC].
- **The ESM-2 screen is one model at one size, on one band of sequence space.** §3.8 used `esm2_t30_150M_UR50D` only; 650M (~2.6 GB fp32) does not fit alongside ESMFold's ~13.8 GB on this card. Every one of the 3,197 genomes scored sits below the native sequence's likelihood, so the screen tests the model where it is least likely to be calibrated, and it scores moves the model did not propose — it cannot bound what an ESM-2-proposed move would do [E2S Scope].
- **Environment.** Shared node; an Ollama fault stopped E seed 4, re-run after a restart with no verdict changed [SEQ §9.12–9.13]; the same fault class recurred once on the agent-memory branch (arm O seed 2, `generate()` hung with the model resident at 0% GPU utilisation), diagnosed the same way and re-run clean after another restart, again with no verdict changed [`results/raw/agent_memory_o_run.log`, `agent_memory_O_seed2.error.txt`].

## 7. Open questions and possible directions (options, not recommendations)

1. **Measure worker scaling on the sequence task.** (a) One ESMFold copy per worker needs ~13.7 GB each [SEQ §6]. (b) Batching folds inside one predictor fits one GPU but changes what N means relative to Eq. 3/7. (c) Getting fp16 or autocast to work would shrink the predictor, but fp16 currently gives 100% NaN [ESM §4], autocast is untried [ESM §6], and fitness accuracy is at risk.
2. **More seeds on A–E.** Six seeds allow p=0.03125 only if every seed agrees (2×0.5⁶, arithmetic). One C run took 2875–3859 s and one D run 4657–4922 s [SEQ §3 tbl 6, §9.7].
3. **Attribute the GA's edge over random search** by ablating selection, crossover and mutation [SEQ §5]; cheap for deterministic arms, costly for LLM arms.
4. **Fix operator variation**: ban or randomise the modal cut, change temperature, or draw the cut in code and leave the LLM content decisions. Each moves the operator toward the deterministic one, the comparison that has so far shown no difference [P2 §5].
5. **Give the LLM something to reason about**, such as structural feedback in the prompt. That tests a different hypothesis and risks leaking the target, which current prompts do not name [SEQ §9.14 item 4].
6. **Compare at equal wall time** (favours A and B given the 4–5x cost [SEQ §5]), or **vary model or population size**, both single points here, at higher cost per call.
7. **Apply the affordance fix to circle proposals** (`propose` fell back 9.2% [SEQ §9.11]); P3 §8 argues it would not change the fitness picture. **This prediction is now directly confirmed by an analogous result:** the agent-memory arms' own affordance-style fix (M2b's explicit-list format, which fixed M2's fallback from a worst-seed 20/38 to 0/38) also did not change the fitness picture [AM3B §2; ODM].
8. **Widen or change the oracle's move class.** Arm O tried only 8 single-position substitutions per slot per step; a larger search width, a richer move class (multi-position edits, structural moves), or a cheaper way to spend the same evaluation budget were not tried, and any of them could change which allocation — more search per step, or more breeding steps — wins [ODM Limits]. Multi-position, segment and indel moves were since tested in a plain GA with no LLM (§3.7), where they changed the hit rate but not final fitness, but not as the oracle's candidates, so this question stays open [MC].
9. **Decide whether to build the ESM-2 operator arm.** The screen in §3.8 found no relationship between ESM-2 150M's preference and realised fitness gain on 3,990 existing moves, which weakens the arm's central assumption before any code is written [E2S]. It does not settle it: the screen measures the bulk of the distribution, while an argmax operator proposes its extreme tail. Deciding it would mean either running the arm anyway (E1 argmax, E2 sampled, against S1 as the matched control, 3 seeds — the per-proposal cost is measured at 0.39 s for a masked batch of 63, about 104 s per run against ~1,000 s of folding, and the 150M model's 774 MiB peak fits the ~1.4 GB headroom left by ESMFold, though co-residency under load was never tested), or dropping it on the screen's evidence. No decision is recorded here.
10. **Directly test "selection is doing the work."** No arm anywhere in this project holds crossover/mutation fixed and varies only selection; the memory and oracle results are consistent with that reading but do not establish it [ODM Limits]. This would also sharpen open question 3's attribution.
