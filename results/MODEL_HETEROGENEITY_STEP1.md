# Model heterogeneity, step 1: format compliance and choice diversity across models

*Branch `model-heterogeneity` (from `main` at `b4450e0`). No GA, no fitness, no ESMFold: this only runs the existing sequence compliance probe (`experiments/probe_sequence_operator_compliance.py`, unchanged) on four models. Driver: `experiments/run_model_heterogeneity_probe.py`; tables generated by `experiments/summarize_model_heterogeneity.py` from the raw files in `results/raw/` (`sequence_operator_compliance_<model>_len63_hetero.json`, `llm_operator_calls_hetero_<model>.jsonl`, `model_heterogeneity_meta.json`, console and driver logs). ## What the tables say

**Which models comply with the formats at all.** `gemma4:12b` and `qwen2.5:7b` comply completely: 0/100 fallback on both operators, 1.00 requests per call. `mistral:7b` mostly complies (4/100 fallback on mutate, 13/100 on crossover, about 1.4 requests per call). `llama3.2:3b` follows the crossover format (2/100 fallback, 1.63 requests per call) but not the mutate format: 41/100 mutate calls fell back after 2.66 requests per call, and 13 mutate outputs changed nothing. So compliance depends on the model, and the 3B model is at the edge.

**Do different models choose different positions?** Yes. All six model pairs differ in position distribution (total-variation distance 0.44 to 0.95, permutation p at the floor of 0.0002 for every pair). The modal positions are 10 (gemma4:12b, 38%), 32 (llama3.2:3b, 23.7%), 10 (qwen2.5:7b, 55%) and 48 (mistral:7b, 6.2%). Individually the models use 17, 21, 6 and 40 of the 63 positions; pooled, 51 of 63 are used by at least one model. Two caveats. gemma4:12b and qwen2.5:7b share their favourite position (10) and are the closest pair (TV 0.44, 6 of 17 positions in common). And concentration is not equal: qwen2.5:7b is the most collapsed (6 positions, 78% on the top three), while mistral:7b is nearly uniform on coverage (40 distinct positions against 49.4 expected under a uniform draw; chi-square 101, p=0.0016, so still not uniform).

**Do different models choose different cuts?** Barely. Every model's modal cut is 40, and pooled over the four models only 10 of 99 possible values were ever used, with 40 alone accounting for 366 of 583 cuts (63%). The models differ only in what they add: gemma4:12b adds 50 (two segments), `llama3.2:3b` and `mistral:7b` mostly declare three segments and add a second cut at 80 (with a few at 60, 20, 85), `qwen2.5:7b` uses 40 alone (100 of 100). For gemma4:12b, PHASE3_RESULTS.md §9.3 traced the 40 to a number in the prompt's prose sentence; whether that is also why the other three models pick 40 was not tested here (it would take the same prompt ablation per model), but every model received the same prompt.

**Per-prompt agreement** is only measurable for one pair. A fallback consumes extra draws from the probe's seeded RNG and desynchronises the stream, so pairs involving `llama3.2:3b` or `mistral:7b` share 0 to 10 prompts and say nothing. `gemma4:12b` and `qwen2.5:7b` (no fallbacks) saw the same 100 genomes: they chose the same position on 33% of them against 23% when one model's answers are shuffled across genomes, and the same (position, letter) on 1%. On the same 100 parent pairs their cut sets were identical on 82%, the same as the shuffled baseline (82%), because qwen2.5:7b always says 40 and gemma4:12b says 40 in 82% of calls.

**Answer to the question.** Heterogeneity does not buy nothing on positions: the models' mutation-position distributions are clearly different and their union covers far more of the genome than any one of them (51 of 63 against 6 to 40). It buys almost nothing on crossover cuts: 40 dominates every model. Two limits on how far to read this. This step measures only what each operator chooses, not whether a mixture of models searches better; a wider spread of positions could be worth little if the extra positions are not useful ones, and step 1 cannot say. And the position difference is partly a compliance difference: the two models that spread positions most (`mistral:7b`, `llama3.2:3b`) are also the two that fall back most often, so some of their spread may be noise rather than a considered choice.

**Memory and co-residency.** Resident sizes at `num_ctx` 4096: 7.5 GiB (gemma4:12b), 2.4 GiB (llama3.2:3b), 4.4 GiB (qwen2.5:7b), 4.6 GiB (mistral:7b), all fully on the GPU. All six pairs loaded together, stayed resident and fully on the GPU, and the first model of each pair was still resident when re-touched (no eviction). The largest pair, gemma4:12b + mistral:7b, used 13,015 MiB of 15,360 MiB. This was tested with a 4-token warm-up per model, not with generation running on both at once. It also leaves no room for ESMFold, which needs ~13.7 GB in fp32 (SEQUENCE_GA_REPORT.md §6 item 8), so the swap between the LLM side and the fold side used in arms C, D and E would still apply. Load times were 9.9 to 14.7 s (Ollama `load_duration`, after unloading everything; the disk cache may have been warm).

## Limits of this probe

- One run per model, 100 calls per operator, one seed, temperature 0.7. `llama3.2:3b` has only 59 successful mutates, so its position distribution rests on 59 observations and 63 cells.
- The probe's own RNG stream is shared across calls, so models are not compared on identical inputs except where neither falls back (above).
- `gemma4:12b` reproduces its earlier results at this sample size (modal position 10 at 38.0% against 37.3% in PHASE3_RESULTS.md §9.2; cut 40 in 82% of cuts against 79 to 83% in §9.3), which is a consistency check on the probe, not on the other models.
- Latencies here (about 1 to 6 s per call) are for a warm model fully on the GPU and are not comparable with the wall-time splits in SEQUENCE_GA_REPORT.md, which include GPU swaps and reloads.
